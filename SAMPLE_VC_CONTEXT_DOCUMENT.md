# HealthTech AI - Founder Call Notes
Date: November 24, 2025
Meeting: First pitch call with Sarah Chen (CEO) and Michael Rodriguez (CTO)

## Company Overview
HealthTech AI is building an AI-powered diagnostic assistant that helps emergency room doctors make faster, more accurate diagnoses. They've developed a computer vision model that analyzes X-rays, CT scans, and patient vitals in real-time to suggest potential diagnoses ranked by probability.

## Founders
- Sarah Chen (CEO): Former ER doctor at Stanford Medical for 8 years. MD from Johns Hopkins. Saw firsthand how overworked ER doctors miss critical details.
- Michael Rodriguez (CTO): Ex-Google Brain researcher. Published 12 papers on medical imaging AI. Previously built fraud detection at Stripe.

## Market Opportunity
- $45B TAM in emergency medicine software
- 6,000+ hospitals in the US alone
- Average ER sees 200+ patients/day, doctors make 5-10 diagnosis errors per shift
- Current solutions (Epic, Cerner) don't have AI diagnostic capabilities

## Traction
- Pilot at 3 hospitals (Stanford, UCSF, UCLA Medical)
- Processing 500+ scans per day
- 94% accuracy rate (vs 87% doctor baseline)
- Average diagnosis time reduced from 45 minutes to 12 minutes
- 2 hospitals already committed to $250K/year contracts starting Q1 2026

## Business Model
- SaaS pricing: $500/month per ER bay (typical hospital has 20-30 bays)
- Average contract value: $150K-$200K per year per hospital
- 95% gross margins (cloud inference costs minimal)
- 18-month sales cycle typical for hospital software

## Fundraising
- Raising $5M seed round at $25M pre-money valuation
- $2.3M committed from General Catalyst and Andreessen Bio
- Need $2.7M more to close round
- Use of funds: 60% sales/BD, 30% R&D, 10% ops

## Team Strengths
- Sarah has deep medical credibility - ER doctors trust her
- Michael's AI expertise is world-class
- Strong advisory board: 3 hospital CMOs, 2 FDA experts
- Team worked together at Stanford for 2 years before founding

## Concerns & Red Flags
- FDA approval path unclear - currently using "clinical decision support" loophole
- Hospital sales cycles are LONG and unpredictable
- Epic/Cerner could build this feature easily
- Only tested in academic medical centers, not rural/community hospitals
- Burn rate is $350K/month - 14 months runway left

## Competitive Landscape
- Zebra Medical (acquired by Nanox) - focused on radiology, not ER
- Aidoc - similar space but $100M+ raised, moving upmarket
- PathAI - pathology focused, not real-time diagnostics
- Key differentiation: Real-time inference + ER-specific workflow integration

## Key Quotes from Call
Sarah: "Every minute matters in the ER. We're not replacing doctors, we're giving them superpowers."
Michael: "Our model has seen 10 million medical images. No individual doctor can match that pattern recognition."
Sarah: "We've had cases where our AI caught pulmonary embolisms that doctors initially missed. It's literally life-saving."

## Next Steps
1. Clinical validation data review - need to see full FDA submission plan
2. Customer reference calls with pilot hospitals
3. Due diligence on competitive moat vs Epic/Cerner
4. Financial model deep dive - unit economics look great but need to validate sales cycle assumptions
5. Technical due diligence on model robustness and bias testing

## Investment Thesis
Strong founding team with perfect founder-market fit. Real traction in a massive market. Technology works and customers are paying. Key risks are regulatory and competition from incumbents, but 3-5 year window before incumbents catch up. If they can sign 50 hospitals in next 18 months, this could be a $1B+ outcome.

Preliminary recommendation: PROCEED with standard seed diligence process.
